# UAE Leasing Inventory: How Dubai & Sharjah Brokerages Hold and Organise Stock — A Data-Model Reference for PropCRM

## TL;DR
- **All three tenure models coexist and must be modelled as first-class, per-scope tenure types**, but the dominant real-world pattern in Dubai and Sharjah is the **agent/management model** (company acts for the owner and takes a fee), with a well-established **head-lease/master-lease** minority (the company becomes the landlord and keeps the margin) and **owned** stock concentrated in developer-landlords (e.g., Tiger, Emaar) and family portfolios. A single firm routinely mixes all three, and tenure can vary **within one building, floor by floor or unit by unit** — so tenure must attach to the lowest inventory node (unit/floor), not the building.
- **Ejari is the anchor for the data model in Dubai.** The unified tenancy contract separates an **"Owner" field (title-deed holder)** from a **"Lessor" field (the party actually granting the lease)**. In a head-lease/managed structure the **management or head-lease company is recorded as Lessor while the title owner sits in the Owner field**, enabled by a RERA **property-management contract registered on Ejari** (plus a mandatory building "leasing details upload"). Sharjah is different: contracts are **attested/registered with Sharjah Municipality** (not Ejari), and under **Law No. 5 of 2024 (issued 23 Sept 2024) subletting in Sharjah is strictly prohibited** except via a Municipality-ratified "investment contract" — which changes what tenure types are even legal there.
- **Leasing commission is a market convention, not a legal tariff:** per Keyper, "5% of the first year's annual rent, plus 5% VAT" — e.g., on AED 100,000 rent, AED 5,000 + AED 250 VAT = **AED 5,250 upfront**, with a common **minimum of ~AED 5,000**, paid by the tenant for residential; commercial runs higher (Springfield Properties: "5% to 10% of annual rent, depending on the property type and lease terms"). In a **head-lease** the company charges the tenant no agency commission on its own stock — it simply keeps rent minus the head rent. In the **management** model the firm takes a **management fee of ~5–8% of annual rent (residential) / ~7–10% (commercial)**, often plus a separate tenant-placement fee.

## Key Findings

1. **Three tenure models, and they mix freely.** UAE brokerages hold lettable stock as (a) **owned**, (b) **head-leased/master-leased** (company leases the whole building/floors, sublets, keeps margin, bears void risk — it IS the landlord to the tenant), or (c) **managed/agency** (company finds tenants and collects rent for an owner, taking a fee). The management model is the market default; head-lease is a distinct, named service offered by established firms (Rocky Real Estate markets a dedicated "Head Lease" service; Betterhomes operates guaranteed-rent/master-lease arrangements). Tenure can vary within one tower.

2. **Head-lease/master-lease terms** in the UAE are commercially negotiated (not statutorily fixed): multi-year terms (commonly 3–10 years for commercial master leases), fixed or indexed rent escalations, a security deposit, and — critically — allocation of **service charges, chiller/district-cooling costs and maintenance** that by Dubai law default to the **owner** unless the contract shifts them. Break clauses are contractual; early-exit convention is ~2 months' rent.

3. **Firm portfolio descriptions vary in transparency.** Most public material blurs owned vs. head-leased vs. managed. Rocky Real Estate explicitly separates a head-lease service from management; Tiger Group is primarily a developer-owner that also leases its own stock; Asteco/Betterhomes are large third-party managers.

4. **Ejari (Dubai) records Lessor separately from Owner**, and a RERA property-management contract must be registered on Ejari before a company can transact tenancies for units it does not own. **Sharjah uses Municipality attestation**, a 4% housing fee (minimum AED 500) via SEWA, and prohibits subletting under Law No. 5 of 2024.

5. **Leasing inventory is building-first but tenure is unit-level.** Leasing units carry attributes irrelevant to sales units (chiller type & who pays, parking allocation, furnishing, cheques accepted, rent-free period, Ejari status, current tenancy end date).

6. **Commission/fee structures**: ~5% + 5% VAT tenant-paid convention (residential); commercial 5–10%; head-lease = margin not commission; management fee 5–10%.

## Details

### 1. The three tenure models and how they mix

**(a) OWNED.** The firm holds the title deed and lets directly. In Dubai this includes developer-landlords who retain stock and family offices that buy floors/units to rent. **Tiger Group / Tiger Properties** (founded 1976, headquartered in Sharjah, active in Dubai and Sharjah) is the archetypal developer-owner: it has developed roughly 200+ projects, ~23,000 units and ~79–80m sq ft of built-up area, and leases its own stock through affiliated entities such as **Al Sabeel Pvt. Real Estate Management (Sharjah)** — "licensed and based in Sharjah… for providing Real Estate Management and Facilities Management to Tiger Group's Private Real Estate" — and **Al Sabil Real Estate Broker (Dubai)** for brokerage/leasing. This is the owned-plus-in-house-management pattern.

**(b) HEAD LEASE / MASTER LEASE.** The company leases an entire building or block of floors from the owner at a fixed head rent for a term, then sublets units to end tenants and keeps the spread; it is the landlord to the tenant and bears void/vacancy risk. This is a recognised, marketed service in Dubai:
- **Rocky Real Estate** runs a dedicated **"Head Lease Services"** line. Verbatim from its site: *"Entrust the assurance of being a full-time landlord to us through Rocky Real Estate's head lease services. Enjoy all the benefits of leasing and complete property management services."* Rocky's public figures are internally inconsistent across pages — its own materials variously cite **"Managing 700+ Buildings and 11,000+ Units"** and, elsewhere, **"700+ Buildings managed and rented; 23,000+ Units rented 'til date"** with **~96% occupancy on its managed leasing portfolio** and **~AED 5–7bn assets under management**. (The 11,000+ figure appears to be current units under management; the 23,000+ figure is cumulative units rented over time — treat both as self-reported and reconcile with the firm directly.)
- **Betterhomes** operates guaranteed-rent/master-lease arrangements. Verbatim: *"when an agency such as Betterhomes obtains the master lease for an entire building and then subleases the apartments to tenants, every tenant in the building owns a sublease contract with Better Homes."* Betterhomes reports **8,500+ units and 65+ buildings under management**.
- **Dania Property Management** explicitly markets "head lease solutions that offer landlords guaranteed income," managing thousands of units across Dubai — confirming head-lease is a distinct commercial product.

The generic label for this in the trade is a **"guaranteed rental"**: a manager rents a block of time/space from the owner, guarantees the owner a sum, and keeps retail letting revenue — "popular with parties seeking to shift the risk and/or management burden associated with renting property."

**(c) AGENT / MANAGEMENT.** The company acts for the owner: markets, screens tenants, draws up the tenancy, registers Ejari, collects rent and remits it to the owner's account, and takes a management fee/commission. This is the market default and is offered by essentially every large firm — **Asteco** (Middle East's largest manager), **Betterhomes**, **Savills**, **Allsopp & Allsopp**, **H&H Property Management & Leasing**, etc. H&H describes actively managing "at all levels, including rent collection, managing leases prudently, and carefully monitoring all third-party contracts." Rocky's management workflow is explicit: "All rental cheques will be collected and deposited into the Property Owner's account by our Property Administrator."

**Can one firm mix all three?** Yes — this is normal. A firm may own some floors, head-lease others, and manage single units for third-party owners simultaneously.

**Can tenure vary WITHIN a single building?** Yes. Your example — a firm owns floors 1–5, head-leases floors 6–10, and manages a single flat for a third-party owner in the same tower — is entirely plausible. **This is the single most important structural fact for the data model: tenure is a property of the lowest inventory node (unit, or a floor-group of units), not of the building.**

**Is it common to buy a floor from a developer (e.g., Emaar) and lease it out?** Yes. Dubai has extensive **freehold commercial floor-plate/office ownership** (DIFC, Business Bay, Emaar Square/Business Park), and whole floors and offices are bought by investors and then leased. Grade-A office investors in Business Bay/DIFC target roughly 7–9% yields on leased investment offices. So "buy a floor, then let it" is a standard owned-tenure acquisition path.

### 2. What a UAE head lease / master lease agreement contains

There is **no statutory master-lease template**; terms are commercially negotiated within the general tenancy law (Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007 as amended by Law No. 33 of 2008; Sharjah Law No. 5 of 2024). Standard components:

- **Term length.** Commercial/master leases commonly run **3–10 years** (Genie AI's UAE 10-year lease template and market guidance both reflect long commercial terms; residential base leases are typically 1 year). A head lease is long precisely so the operator can amortise fit-out/void risk.
- **Rent escalation.** Escalation is **explicitly written into the contract** (fixed % step-ups or index-linked). For end-tenant subleases, in-term increases are constrained by the **RERA rental index** and require **90 days' notice** before renewal (Decree No. 43 of 2013); the head lease itself is negotiated between commercial parties.
- **Security deposit.** A deposit is standard; for end-tenancies the convention is **5% of annual rent (unfurnished) / 10% (furnished)**. Dubai law "does not explicitly regulate security deposits," so the contract governs who holds it and refund timing (commonly 14–21 days after handover). In a head lease, the operator posts a deposit to the owner and separately holds deposits from its sub-tenants.
- **Service charges & chiller/utilities.** By default under **Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019** and DLD guidance, **owners are primarily responsible for jointly-owned-property service charges and common-area chiller/district-cooling costs unless the lease expressly shifts them**. "Even where the lease does so, the owner remains legally liable towards the management entity if the tenant does not pay." Chiller consumption is frequently pushed to the occupier — the RDC has enforced tenant liability for chiller consumption where the contract so provides (a tenant was ordered to pay AED 250,000 in chiller charges for six months). A head lease must therefore specify: who pays master-community/service charges, who pays chiller demand vs. consumption, and who pays the fuel surcharge.
- **Maintenance.** Default: landlord/owner bears major maintenance; minor maintenance can be shifted by contract. Under RERA norms the landlord maintains common services (Law No. 26 of 2007, Arts. 4/16). A head-lease operator typically takes on day-to-day maintenance and tenant-facing repairs while major structural stays with the owner.
- **Break clauses.** Purely contractual. Where absent, early exit convention is **~2 months' rent plus possible deposit forfeiture**, and the RDSC may award further damages for the remaining term (mitigated if re-let).
- **Registration.** The **head lease/management relationship is registered with DLD via Ejari** — specifically through a **property-management contract registration** (see §4) and the **building "leasing details upload."** In Sharjah, the master/investment lease is attested at the **Municipality** and, under Law No. 5 of 2024, must be certified within **15 days**.

### 4. Ejari and who is recorded as landlord

**Dubai — the Owner vs. Lessor distinction is the crux.** The **Unified Ejari Tenancy Contract** contains a section "Owner / Lessor Information" with **two separate fields: "Owner's Name" (from the title deed) and "Lessor's Name" (the party actually granting the lease).** In a head-lease or managed structure, **the head-lease/management company is entered as the Lessor while the title-deed holder remains the Owner.** As one practitioner guide puts it: "The lessor name is who is actually leasing it… If a property management company is signing on behalf of the owner, their trade license number goes in the license field."

For the sub-tenant, the contractual landlord is the company, not the owner: per Betterhomes, when an agency holds the master lease and subleases, "every tenant in the building owns a sublease contract with Better Homes."

**The enabling registration is a RERA property-management contract on Ejari.** Before a company can register tenancies for units it does not own, it must register the **"management contract between owners and management companies"** via DLD's Ejari e-service. DLD eligibility: the company must be "licensed for one of the real estate activities related to tenancy," and be "the owner of the property **or their representative as per an official power of attorney or a signed management contract.**" Once registered, "the company gains direct system access for all tenancy-related transactions across the landlord's portfolio" (EGSH, a DLD-authorised Trustee Centre).

**Prerequisite: building "leasing details upload."** "Before any tenancy contract can be registered in Ejari, the property must exist in the DLD's leasing database" — requiring the DLD-approved unit list, certified floor plans, ownership evidence, and (if a representative applies) a POA or DLD-recognised management agreement.

**Documents required (Dubai):**
- *To register the management/head-lease relationship:* RERA/DED trade licence for a real-estate/management activity; ownership proof (title deed/Oqood); the **signed management contract or POA**; authorised system user.
- *To register each sub-tenancy Ejari:* signed unified tenancy contract; tenant Emirates ID/passport (+visa; for companies, trade licence & MOA); title deed / DLD property number; **DEWA premises number**; security-deposit receipt; POA if a representative signs. Written **owner consent/NOC** is required for subletting, and the sublease cannot exceed the head-lease term (Law No. 26 of 2007, Arts. 24–25).
- **Separate registration for the head lease itself:** Yes in effect — the master relationship is captured through the **management-contract registration** + building upload; there is no consumer-facing "head lease" product name, but the mechanism exists.

**Terminology caveat (flagged):** DLD's official, verifiable terms are **"property/real-estate management contract"** and **"building leasing details upload."** Phrases like *"Ejari for sublease," "investment tenancy contract,"* or *"investment lease"* appear in **brokerage/blog marketing, not on DLD/RERA pages** — treat them as informal. Also, sources conflate two legally distinct structures: (i) pure **agency/management** (owner stays the economic landlord; company registers via management contract/POA) and (ii) a true **commercial head-lease** (company is itself the owner's tenant and sublets for its own account). DLD's management-contract vehicle squarely covers (i); for (ii) the practical Ejari route is the same, but there is **no single official DLD sentence** stating a for-profit head-lessee is the registered lessor — this is the least-documented point and should be treated as inferred-but-well-supported.

**Sharjah — a different system entirely.** Sharjah has **no Ejari**; tenancy contracts are **attested/registered with Sharjah Municipality's Rental Regulation Department** (increasingly via the "Aqari"/electronic platform). Key differences:
- Landlord must **certify the lease within 15 days** of issuance (Law No. 5 of 2024, issued 23 Sept 2024 by Sheikh Dr. Sultan bin Muhammad Al Qasimi); per the Sharjah Government Media Office, "Landlords in Sharjah are obliged to ratify rental contracts within 15 days of issuance." A certified contract is an **"executive writ"** enforceable at the RDRC.
- A **4% housing/municipality fee** on annual rent (minimum AED 500) is collected **monthly via the SEWA bill** (contrast Dubai's 5% housing fee via DEWA).
- **Subletting is strictly prohibited** under Law No. 5 of 2024 (a ground for eviction), **except** via a Municipality-ratified **"investment contract"** — the Municipality can "ratify an investment contract where the tenant is allowed to sub-lease for a period… with the approval of the property owner," requiring the sub-lessor's commercial licence, the title deed, the map, and the printed investment contract. So in Sharjah, the head-lease/sublet model is only legal through this specific **investment-contract** mechanism; ordinary tenant subletting is banned. Charging tenants a **renewal fee is also illegal** under the 2024 law.
- Sharjah's 2024 reform also opened property ownership to **all nationalities**, and established a dedicated **Rental Disputes Centre (Law No. 6 of 2024)**.

For the data model: **the "landlord/lessor of record" is a distinct entity from "owner," and the registration authority + document set differ by emirate.** PropCRM must store both parties and the registration-authority context per unit/contract.

### 5. Leasing inventory in practice

**Building-first, unit-beneath — but tenure at unit/floor level.** Firms think **building → floor → unit**, because they operate whole buildings (rent rolls, shared systems, one fire-safety AMC, service charges via Mollak in jointly-owned Dubai buildings). Betterhomes describes whole-building management as "a single asset, with one rent roll, shared systems and an ongoing compliance schedule." But because tenure can differ per unit, the model needs a **Building** entity, a **Floor** grouping, and a **Unit** entity that carries its own **tenure record** (owned / head-leased / managed), its own **owner**, its own **lessor**, and — for head-leased/managed units — a link to the governing **head-lease or management contract**.

**Attributes that matter for a leasing unit but NOT for a sales unit:**
- **Chiller/district-cooling**: provider (Empower/Emicool/none), chiller-free vs. chiller-paid, and **who pays** (landlord-absorbed vs. tenant consumption + demand + fuel surcharge). Chiller can account for 20–30% of monthly utility cost, so "chiller-free" is a first-class marketing/filter flag.
- **Parking**: number of allocated bays and whether included.
- **Furnishing status**: furnished / semi / unfurnished (drives 10% vs 5% deposit convention).
- **Number of cheques accepted**: 1/2/4/6/12 (a core negotiation lever and CRM filter — fewer cheques strengthens the tenant's price position).
- **Rent-free period / incentives**: free month(s), no-commission offers.
- **Ejari status**: registered / pending / not registered / cancelled (Dubai); **Municipality attestation status** (Sharjah).
- **Current tenancy end date / lease expiry & renewal window** (90-day notice tracking).
- **Rent, deposit held, deposit holder**, permitted use (residential/commercial/trade-licence match for commercial), maximum occupancy/co-occupant registration (2025–26 Dubai rule), service-charge liability split, maintenance responsibility split, and — for head-leased units — the **head rent payable to the owner** (to compute margin and void exposure).

**Typical portfolio size for a mid-size Dubai leasing firm:** highly variable. Large players report **8,500+ units / 65+ buildings (Betterhomes)**, **700+ buildings / 11,000+ units (Rocky)**, **~32,000 units on the Asteco/Aldar Estates platform**. A **mid-size** firm sits well below these — on the order of a few hundred to a few thousand units under management across tens of buildings. (This is an inference from the reported ranges of named firms; there is no authoritative published median for "mid-size," so treat as indicative.)

### 6. Commission and fee structures in leasing

- **Residential leasing commission (agent/management):** market convention is **~5% of annual rent, minimum ~AED 5,000, plus 5% VAT**, **paid by the tenant**, due on signing. Per Keyper: "The rate is standardised across the market: 5% of the first year's annual rent, plus 5% VAT on that commission… For a property with AED 100,000 annual rent, the agency fee is AED 5,000 + AED 250 VAT = AED 5,250 paid upfront." It is **not a legal cap** — RERA recognises it as a customary, negotiable practice, not legislation; an agent asking 6% is not breaking a law, and renewals are often reduced or waived if no new work is done.
- **Commercial leasing commission:** higher and more variable. Per Springfield Properties: "For commercial leases, commission may range between **5% to 10% of annual rent**, depending on the property type and lease terms." Longer leases and exclusive mandates justify higher rates.
- **Sharjah:** the same ~5% brokerage convention is used in practice for finding tenants, **but** note the distinct fee environment: a **4% municipality housing fee** (minimum AED 500, via SEWA), **charging tenants a renewal fee is illegal** under Law No. 5 of 2024, and subletting is banned outside the investment-contract route. Confirm the exact commission with the Sharjah brokerage as it is convention, not statute.
- **Head-lease model — how the money works:** because the company **is the landlord**, it does **not** charge itself/its tenant an agency commission on its own head-leased stock. Its revenue is the **spread**: sub-tenant rent collected **minus** the fixed head rent paid to the owner, minus voids, service charges/chiller it has agreed to bear, and maintenance. (It may still charge a commission if a third-party agent introduces the tenant, but not as principal.) The owner receives guaranteed rent; the operator keeps the margin and bears void risk.
- **Management (agency) fee:** typically **5–8% of annual rent for long-term residential** and **~7–10% for commercial**; short-term/holiday-home management is far higher (15–25% of revenue). Lower-rent units are often charged a **flat annual fee (~AED 4,000–5,000)**. A **tenant-placement fee** (often one month's rent, or 5% of first year) is frequently billed **on top** of the recurring management percentage, and maintenance mark-ups (10–20%) and renewal/admin fees are common extras. Management fees are market benchmarks, not government tariffs.

## Recommendations (for the PropCRM data model)

**Stage 1 — Model tenure at the unit level as a first-class, time-bounded record.**
- Entities: `Building` → `Floor` (optional grouping) → `Unit`. Attach a **`TenureRecord`** to each Unit (or floor-group), with `tenure_type ∈ {OWNED, HEAD_LEASE, MANAGED}`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `owner_party_id`, `lessor_party_id`, and (for HEAD_LEASE/MANAGED) `governing_contract_id`.
- This directly supports "floors 1–5 owned, 6–10 head-leased, one managed flat" in the same tower. **Do not put tenure on Building.**

**Stage 2 — Separate "Owner" from "Lessor of record" everywhere, mirroring Ejari.**
- On every tenancy, store both `owner_party` (title-deed holder) and `lessor_party` (who signs as landlord — may be your head-lease/management company). Store the **registration authority** (`EJARI_DUBAI` vs `SHARJAH_MUNICIPALITY`) and authority-specific IDs (Ejari contract no. / barcode; Sharjah attestation ref).
- Model a **`ManagementContract` / `HeadLeaseContract`** object (parties, term, head rent, escalation, deposit, service-charge/chiller/maintenance allocation, break clause, DLD management-contract registration status, building-leasing-upload status). Link sub-tenancies to it.

**Stage 3 — Give leasing units their letting-specific attribute set** (chiller type & payer, parking bays, furnishing, cheques accepted, rent-free/incentives, Ejari/attestation status, current tenancy end date, rent, deposit + deposit-holder, service-charge/maintenance split, permitted use, max occupancy). Keep these distinct from sales-unit attributes.

**Stage 4 — Model money by tenure type.**
- MANAGED: `management_fee_pct` (default 5–8% resi / 7–10% comm) + optional `tenant_placement_fee` + `maintenance_markup_pct` + `renewal_fee`; rent flows to owner's account, fee deducted.
- HEAD_LEASE: track `head_rent` (to owner) and `sublease_rent` (from tenant) to compute **margin and void exposure**; no self-charged agency commission.
- OWNED: rent flows to the firm; optional internal management fee for reporting.
- Commission on brokered deals: `commission_pct` (default 5%), `commission_min` (AED 5,000), `vat_pct` (5%), `payer` (default TENANT), all overridable — because these are conventions, not statutes.

**Stage 5 — Make emirate a behavioural switch, not just a field.** For Sharjah units, enforce: Municipality attestation (15-day certification), 4% SEWA housing fee (min AED 500), **subletting disabled unless an "investment contract" tenure flag is set**, no tenant renewal fee. For Dubai, enable Ejari fields, 5% DEWA housing fee, RERA rental-index/90-day-notice tracking, co-occupant registration.

**Benchmarks that would change the design:** if a target customer's book is >~50% head-lease (rarer, but Rocky/Dania-style operators), prioritise margin/void analytics and deposit-chain tracking; if it's dominated by Sharjah stock, prioritise the investment-contract sublet path and Municipality workflows over Ejari.

## Caveats
- **Commission and management-fee percentages are market conventions, not legal tariffs**, and are negotiable; store them as configurable defaults, never hard-coded.
- **The "head-lessee as registered Lessor" mechanism is well-supported but not stated in a single official DLD sentence.** DLD's verifiable vehicle is the **property-management contract registered on Ejari** plus the two-field (Owner/Lessor) unified contract; "investment tenancy contract"/"Ejari for sublease" are informal terms. Validate the exact current Ejari workflow with a DLD Trustee Centre before finalising the registration sub-model.
- **Sharjah subletting is strictly prohibited under Law No. 5 of 2024** except via a Municipality-ratified investment contract — so the head-lease/sublet tenure type is legally constrained in Sharjah in a way it is not in Dubai.
- **"Typical mid-size portfolio size" is an inference** from the ranges published by named large firms; there is no authoritative published median.
- Service-charge/chiller liability **defaults to the owner** under Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 but is frequently shifted by contract; model it as an explicit per-contract allocation rather than assuming a default.
- **Firm-specific unit counts and AUM figures are self-reported marketing figures and may be dated or internally inconsistent** (Rocky, for example, publishes both "11,000+ units" and "23,000+ units rented" on different pages). Verify before relying on them commercially.
