# Resale & Leasing — Practitioner Answers
Received Day 98. Answers to docs/Resale_Practitioner_Questions.md from a practising
secondary-market broker. THIS IS THE SOURCE. The board summarises it; where the two differ,
this document is right.

## 1. Does the brokerage ever hold money?
Yes. The brokerage may temporarily hold a booking amount, depending on the developer's payment and
booking policies. Held temporarily, then transferred or allocated per the applicable transaction or
developer procedure.

## 2. Who pays the broker, and when?
- **Off-plan:** the developer pays the full agreed commission. Nothing from the buyer.
- **Resale:** 2% from the seller and 2% from the buyer when the brokerage represents both sides.
- **Collection:** from both parties **on transfer day at the trustee office**.

## 3. One resale deal, end to end
- **Acquisition:** direct from sellers/leads, or through other brokerages and colleagues.
- **Initial documents:** seller ID and property title deed.
- **Marketing:** social media, and MLS/portals — Dubizzle, Property Finder.
- **Buyer sourcing:** other brokerages, advertising leads, the agent's own connections.
- **Buyer qualification:** the purpose — long-term investor, flipper, or end-user.
- **Form F / MOU signed** — the legal countdown to transfer begins.

Then four phases:
1. **Developer NOC** — the seller applies. The developer verifies service charges and community
   fees are paid and current.
2. **Trustee appointment** — once the NOC and financial terms are ready, booked by a licensed
   agent, a conveyancer, or the parties themselves through DLD channels.
3. **Transfer day** — separate manager's cheques, seller liability clearance, DLD and trustee
   requirements.
4. **Handover** — keys once the required initial amounts are paid and payments are aligned.

Commission norm: at 25% of the property paid, though practice varies.
Mortgage: escrow-account formalities and the bank's own procedures.

## 4. The documents, and who issues them
- **Form A** — seller's listing agreement
- **Form B** — buyer's representation agreement
- **Form F** — the MOU / sale contract. Form F is the official DLD (RERA) name for the unified sale
  and purchase contract; "MOU" is the traditional industry term for the same thing.
- **Developer NOC** — issued by the developer (Emaar, Nakheel, DAMAC). 3–7 working days.

## 5. The NOC
- The **seller** applies to the developer.
- **3–7 working days** to issue.
- Valid roughly **15–30 days** for most developers and DLD trustee offices.
- **If it expires:** the seller reapplies and usually pays a new fee.
- **It stays valid only while:** all dues are clear (service charges, utilities); the buyer, seller
  and property details match the records at the final transfer appointment exactly; and it is used
  for a single transaction within its short lifespan.
- **Fees:** paid by the seller. AED 500–5,000, plus VAT in some cases, depending on the developer.

## 6. Mortgage deals
- **More than 50%** of secondary transactions involve a buyer mortgage.
- Additional requirement: **bank NOC**.
- Most common problem: **delay in obtaining it**.

## 7. The transfer appointment
Booked **1–3 days in advance**. Required: developer NOC · signed Form F / MOU · ID · manager's
cheques · original title deed · mortgage documents or NOC where applicable · power of attorney
where applicable.
If one party does not attend: breach of contract, applicable penalties, and dispute resolution
depending on the circumstances and the contract.

## 8. Where deals are lost
1. **Bank NOC delay** — the bank does not issue in time. ⭐ *"This could be identified earlier if
   the CRM tracked the bank NOC deadline and followed up automatically."*
2. **Buyer does not have the funds** — proof of funds or mortgage pre-approval was not verified
   before Form F was signed. The shortfall should surface early, the developer notified in writing
   where applicable, and the SPA reviewed for grace periods.
3. **Seller changes his mind** — tempted by another offer after signing. Under DLD and RERA rules
   the agent follows the applicable compliance and advisory procedures.

## 9. Leasing — a different job, or a smaller sale?
Documents: unified tenancy contract · Ejari registration · identity and ownership verification ·
financial and payment instruments · Forms A, B and F.
Commission: typically **5% of the annual rent, paid by the tenant**, subject to a minimum flat fee
such as AED 5,000, whichever is higher, plus 5% VAT.
⚠️ **After leasing the brokerage's involvement ENDS** — once the tenancy contract is signed, Ejari
is registered and commission is collected. The rest is handled by a property management company
with its own active RERA agreement.
